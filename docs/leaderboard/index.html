About FPM

What it is

FPM estimates how much a player helps his team outscore the opponent per 100 possessions, once teammates and opposition are accounted for. One two-way number for the EuroLeague, built to measure how good a player is now.

Why “fair”

It blends a regularized on/off rating (RAPM) with an era-neutral, position-aware box-score prior. The box score sets what to expect, the scoreboard says what actually happened, and the blend stays stable and honest. Every setting is chosen by out-of-sample testing, not by hand.

How to read it

Points per 100 possessions versus an average rotation player: one point of FPM is worth about one point per 100 possessions of a lineup’s margin. A season runs from roughly −6 to +7. O-FPM and D-FPM split it into offense and defense. The small figure in each box is the player's percentile for that season, and the cell shades lighter as that rises. ± is the standard error, shown on the current season.

What it accounts for

Luck is taken out by grading expected points from shot quality, not whether shots fell. Each season is judged against its own league, so eras stay comparable. Players are sorted into guard / wing / forward / big tiers with a box-score role tag, and an aging curve and recency weighting keep the rating current.

One caveat

FPM measures impact inside a team and a system, not context-free talent. A strong setup lifts a player's number, so part of a high rating belongs to the situation as much as the player.

Coverage

Regular season and the old Top 16, every season from 2007-08 to today. The current season updates through the year as games are played.
